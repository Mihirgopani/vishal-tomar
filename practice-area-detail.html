Strategic Significance

Judicial Review of State Action

The extraordinary writ jurisdiction of the High Court of Gujarat under Article 226 represents the most potent legal instrument for commercial entities and individuals against arbitrary, discriminatory, or unlawful state conduct.

Advocate Vishalkumar R Tomar regularly initiates and defends Special Civil Applications (SCA) before Single and Division Benches of the High Court. When statutory authorities, public sector undertakings, or municipal corporations violate fundamental rights or disregard natural justice, timely invocation of writ remedies can secure immediate ex-parte stays and status quo protections.

Our chambers approach to writ litigation is founded upon establishing the jurisdictional threshold immediately—demonstrating why alternate remedies are either inefficacious or why the impugned state order is void ab initio under established Wednesbury unreasonableness and proportionality doctrines.

"A successful writ petition requires immediate identification of the constitutional infirmity—whether procedural bias, lack of jurisdiction, or violation of Article 14—crystallized into unanswerable grounds of challenge."

Matters Handled

Spectrum of High Court Writ Challenges

Representative categories of legal challenges regularly argued before the High Court of Gujarat.

Public Tender & Procurement Disputes

Challenging wrongful technical disqualification, tailor-made tender conditions favoring specific bidders, illegal blacklisting, and unlawful encashment of bank guarantees by government departments.

Revenue & Land Ceiling Challenges

Writs challenging arbitrary orders passed by Collectors, Special Secretaries (Revenue Department - SSRD), and land acquisition officers regarding non-agricultural permissions and tenancy determinations.

Statutory & Regulatory Overreach

Petitions challenging the constitutionality or ultra vires nature of delegated rules, notifications, arbitrary licensing cancellations, and retrospective penalties imposed by state agencies.

Service & Employment Disputes

Challenging illegal termination, supersession, denial of promotion, departmental inquiry violations, and pension denials in state boards, universities, and public corporations.

Police Inaction & Fundamental Rights

Writs of Mandamus seeking registration of FIRs in severe commercial frauds, protection petitions for personal liberty, and petitions against arbitrary detention or harassment.

Letters Patent Appeals (LPA)

Intra-court appellate advocacy before Division Benches challenging adverse interim or final rulings delivered by Single Judges in Special Civil Applications.

Common Inquiries

Writ Litigation FAQs

Key procedural questions regarding High Court of Gujarat writ petitions.

Article 226 empowers the High Court to issue prerogative writs (Certiorari, Mandamus, Habeas Corpus, etc.) against the State and public bodies for violation of fundamental rights or statutory obligations. In contrast, Article 227 is the supervisory jurisdiction exercised over subordinate courts and administrative tribunals to correct grave errors of jurisdiction or manifest injustice.
Generally, purely contractual disputes between private parties cannot be resolved through writ petitions. However, where one of the contracting parties is the State, a government instrumentality, or a public authority, and its action is demonstrably arbitrary, discriminatory, or in breach of Article 14, the High Court possesses jurisdiction to intervene and grant relief.
In circumstances of genuine emergency—such as impending demolition, tender award finalization, or encashment of a bank guarantee—counsel can file an urgent Special Civil Application and seek permission from the Bench for same-day or next-day circulation and ad-interim relief.
An LPA is an intra-court appeal provided under Clause 15 of the Letters Patent of the Gujarat High Court. It allows an aggrieved litigant to appeal against a judgment or substantial interim order of a Single Judge to a Division Bench comprising two Judges of the High Court.
