SCOPE OF WRIT JURISDICTION UNDER ARTICLE 226 IN
COMMERCIAL TENDER DISPUTES.

Examining the fine boundary between legitimate judicial restraint and constitutional intervention when state agencies act arbitrarily in government contracts.

Consult on Tender Writ

For commercial enterprises bidding for high-value public procurement, EPC, and infrastructure concessions in Gujarat, arbitrary rejection of technical bids is an existential risk. But when does an administrative decision cross the threshold from commercial discretion into unconstitutional arbitrariness warranting High Court writ intervention?

The fundamental starting point in administrative law has always been that the State, when entering into the marketplace, must be permitted commercial freedom. The courts do not sit as appellate bodies over technical evaluation committees. However, the State does not shed its constitutional personality under Article 14 merely because it is floating a commercial tender.

"The principles of fair play in action apply to government contracts as forcefully as they apply to quasi-judicial actions. The State cannot act arbitrarily, maliciously, or with an oblique motive under the guise of commercial freedom." — Supreme Court of India in Tata Cellular v. Union of India

2. The Landmark Tata Cellular Principles

In the watershed ruling of Tata Cellular v. Union of India (1994) 6 SCC 651, the Supreme Court synthesized the parameters governing judicial review of administrative contracts:

  • Modern judicial review is directed not against the ultimate commercial decision, but against the decision-making process.
  • The government must have freedom of contract; however, a fair play in the joints is a necessary concomitant for an administrative body functioning in an administrative sphere.
  • The decision must not be infected by bias, mala fides, or Wednesbury unreasonableness—where no sensible authority could have ever arrived at such a conclusion.

3. Gujarat High Court Jurisprudence

The High Court of Gujarat has consistently navigated this delicate balance. In numerous Special Civil Applications, Division Benches of the High Court have intervened when public bodies have:

  1. Introduced retrospective qualifications or amended tender requirements after bids were unsealed.
  2. Conditionally accepted non-responsive bids of favored bidders while hyper-technically disqualifying competitors over trivial formatting clericalities.
  3. Invoked coercive bank guarantees when delays were demonstrably occasioned by the employer's failure to hand over encumbrance-free project land.
PRACTICE NOTE: URGENT INTERIM RELIEF

Once work orders are executed and third-party rights crystallize, High Courts are understandably hesitant to unravel contracts due to public interest in uninterrupted public works. Therefore, filing the Special Civil Application immediately upon technical disqualification—and seeking urgent mention before the Bench for stay of financial bid opening—is pivotal.

4. What Constitutes Manifest Arbitrariness

Following the Supreme Court's jurisprudence in Shayara Bano and Silppi Constructions, "manifest arbitrariness" is recognized as an independent ground to strike down state executive action under Article 14. An action is manifestly arbitrary when it is done without any determining principle, capriciously, or without adequate determining factual basis.

5. Strategic Takeaways for Litigants

Parties contemplating High Court writ remedies against arbitrary tender cancellations must ensure three procedural fundamentals before approaching the Bench:

  • Contemporaneous Representations: Submit a detailed factual objection to the tender authority within 24-48 hours of disqualification, creating an irrefutable record of natural justice violation.
  • Establish Public Detriment: Demonstrate that the petitioner’s bid was substantially more competitive, proving that arbitrary exclusion costs the public exchequer millions of rupees.
  • Clean Hands Doctrine: Writ jurisdiction is equitable; the petitioner must make complete, candid disclosure of all correspondence without suppressing prior show-cause notices.
